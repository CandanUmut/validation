# Design Brief — RAN Validation Trainer

> Status: **proposal**. Nothing here is final until Umut approves the Phase 0 mockup (`docs/design/mockup/`). Every choice says *why*, so it can be argued with.

Decided by Umut (2026-11-13): **phone first** · content for **UE validation, RAN/gNB validation, operator work and interview prep** · **research first** · signature direction **signal & spectrum**.

---

## 1. The one idea

**Learn to read the air.**

Everything this job tests happens in a medium nobody can see: radio. A validation engineer's skill is making the invisible readable — a trace, a constellation, a log line, a ladder of messages. The app does the same for what you know: your knowledge is drawn as signals, and practice makes them cleaner.

- **Crafted, not dashboard-y.** Drawings look hand-made by an engineer with a technical pen, not generated by a chart library.
- **Honest, not gamified.** Every visual means something you can explain in one sentence. No decoration pretending to be data.
- **Calm and warm.** It is used every day on a phone, often for ten minutes. It should feel like opening a good notebook.

If a choice makes the app feel like a hacker movie, a trading terminal, or a toy, it is wrong.

---

## 2. What it must never look like

- Generic "AI app": purple-blue gradients, glassmorphism, glowing blobs, emoji icons, rounded cards with heavy shadows.
- "Hacker terminal": neon green on black, matrix rain, glitch effects.
- Telecom clichés: stock photos of cell towers, lightning-bolt "5G" logos, world maps with glowing lines.
- Instrument cosplay (my mistake in the v2 redesign): readouts and gauges that look technical but carry little meaning.
- Gamification noise: confetti, XP bars, badges, streak flames, loot.

## 3. Visual language

### 3.1 Signature: signals you can read

Three drawn forms, each with one clear meaning:

| Form | Where | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **The trace** — a signal line drawn across the top of a screen | Today, chapter pages | Progress. Solid where done, dotted where still ahead, a dot where you are now. |
| **The constellation** — a 16-QAM diagram | Each module, Today | How well you remember a module. Each point is one review card. Well-remembered cards sit on their ideal spot; shaky ones scatter. Like EVM: a clean constellation means a clean link. |
| **The spectrum strip** — modules as bands along a frequency axis | Today, Library | Coverage. Band height = share of the module you have learned. Gaps are visible at a glance. |

Rules:
- One drawn signal per screen as the hero. Others appear small, if at all.
- They draw themselves once on first view (≤ 600 ms), then stay still. Never loop.
- Every drawing has a one-line plain caption saying what it shows.

### 3.2 Supporting forms

- **Ladder diagrams** for message sequences, in the same pen style: hairline lifelines, arrows coloured by protocol layer.
- **Annotated logs.** Log extracts look like printed paper you mark up: tap a line to highlight it in marker yellow, and add a short note in the margin. That is how engineers actually read logs.
- **Spec trail.** Every lesson ends with the exact 3GPP references it rests on (spec, section, release). This is the research-first rule made visible.

### 3.3 Palette (proposal)

Light first: phones are used in daylight. Dark is a full variant, not an afterthought.

| Token | Role | Light | Dark | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `--paper` | background | `#F5F2EA` warm paper | `#11161C` | notebook page |
| `--ink` | text | `#17202A` blue-black | `#E8E6E0` | technical pen ink |
| `--ink-soft` | secondary text | `#59626C` | `#A3A9B0` | |
| `--rule` | hairlines, grids | `#DCD6C8` | `#28303A` | graph paper |
| `--trace` | the one accent | cobalt `#2747C9` | `#93A6FF` | pen ink on a trace; not the overused orange or purple |
| `--marker` | highlights only | `#F7DD72` | `#5C4E14` | highlighter on printed logs |
| `--pass` / `--fail` / `--inconc` | verdicts only | `#2F7A47` / `#B3261E` / `#9A6300` | lighter variants | test verdict colours, never decoration |

Protocol layers keep a small fixed palette (PHY, MAC, RLC, PDCP, RRC, NAS, NGAP/S1AP), used only for thin arrows and tags, always with the layer name next to it.

Rules: one accent per view (`--trace`). Verdict colours only for verdicts. WCAG 2.2 AA contrast in both themes, checked by a script in the build.

### 3.4 Typography (proposal — self-hosted, no runtime CDN)

| Role | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Display | **Archivo** (variable, width axis) | Engineering grotesque; the semi-expanded width reads like a spec-sheet heading without looking like a template. |
| Reading | **Atkinson Hyperlegible Next** | Built for legibility on small screens; clear 0/O, I/l/1, which matters with names like `T310`, `N310`, `RLC`. |
| Logs, messages, spec refs | **JetBrains Mono** | Unambiguous characters; good at small sizes for log lines. |

Rules: reading measure 60–70 characters, body 17px on phone, line height 1.6. No all-caps headings; small caps only for short labels.

### 3.5 Form

- Hairline borders, 6–8px corners, no heavy shadows. Depth from paper layers and space.
- A faint graph-paper grid only behind drawings, never behind text.
- Icons: one custom set, drawn in the same pen weight as the traces.

---

## 4. Phone-first rules (non-negotiable)

- Tap targets ≥ 48px. Primary actions in thumb reach (lower half of the screen).
- Nothing depends on hover or a keyboard. Keyboard support may exist quietly on desktop; it is never a feature to advertise.
- Works one-handed: bottom navigation, no tiny close buttons in the top corner for key flows.
- Readable at 17px body without zoom; nothing scrolls sideways except logs and ladders, which say so.
- Offline after the first visit. Fast on a mid-range phone: no framework runtime on reading pages.

---

## 5. Signature screens

1. **Today** — the trace (this week's practice), three things to do with honest time estimates, the constellation of your weakest module.
2. **Lesson** — reading page with a drawing, "say it in one breath", "watch out", and the spec trail.
3. **Journey chapter** — the ladder diagram you predict, fill and rebuild (the three passes stay).
4. **Case file** — annotated log, decisions as a debug trail, root cause, bug title.
5. **Review** — one card at a time, big text, three grade buttons in thumb reach.

---

## 6. Motion

- Drawings draw once; text never animates.
- Page changes: a short fade (≤ 200 ms).
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: no drawing animation at all.

---

## 7. Voice

- Plain words. Short sentences. Explain like a senior engineer to a colleague, not like a textbook.
- Say when something is uncertain or simplified. Never claim more than the sources say.
