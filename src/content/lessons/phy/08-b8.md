title: Uplink power control and timing advance
source: v1
points:
  - "Uplink power: <code>P = min(Pcmax, P0 + alpha x PL + 10log10(M) + delta + f)</code>. P0 is the target per RB, alpha is how much path loss is compensated, M is RBs, f is closed-loop TPC commands."
  - Typical phone maximum is <b>23 dBm</b> (power class 3), which is 0.2 W.
  - <b>PHR</b> (power headroom report) tells the base station how much power the phone has left. Zero or negative headroom means the phone is power-limited.
  - "<b>Timing advance (TA)</b>: the phone sends early so its uplink arrives aligned with other phones. The first TA comes in the random access response. Later updates come as MAC control elements."
  - LTE TA step is 16 Ts = 0.52 &micro;s, about 78 m of distance. The maximum initial TA of 1282 covers about 100 km of cell radius.
  - If <code>timeAlignmentTimer</code> expires, the phone is out of uplink sync and must do random access again.
say: Power control keeps the received uplink level right. Timing advance keeps uplink arrival times aligned inside the cyclic prefix.
trap: The standard LTE timing advance range stops near 100 km. A satellite is hundreds of kilometers away. That is one of the core direct-to-cell problems.
