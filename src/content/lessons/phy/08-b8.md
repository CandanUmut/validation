title: Uplink power control and timing advance
source: research
points:
- 'NR PUSCH power: <code>P = min{Pcmax, P0 + 10·log10(2<sup>μ</sup>·M_RB) + α·PL + Δ_TF + f}</code>. P0 is the target, α how much path loss is compensated, M_RB the allocated RBs, f the closed-loop TPC state.'
- The common UE maximum is <b>23 dBm</b> (power class 3, 0.2 W).
- '<b>PHR</b> reports headroom: the gap between the UE''s maximum power and the power its uplink would need. Zero or negative means power-limited.'
- <b>Timing advance:</b> the UE transmits early so its uplink arrives aligned. The first value comes in the random access response (NR index 0–3846), later corrections as MAC CEs (0–63).
- 'Step size is 16·64·Tc/2<sup>μ</sup>: 0.52 µs at 15 kHz (about 78 m of distance), halving with each step up in spacing. In LTE the step is 16 Ts and N_TA ≤ 20512 Ts, about 100 km of cell radius.'
- If <code>timeAlignmentTimer</code> expires the UE flushes HARQ, releases PUCCH and SRS, and may send nothing but a random access preamble until it is aligned again.
say: Power control keeps the received uplink level right; timing advance keeps uplink arrival inside the cyclic prefix.
trap: The LTE timing-advance range ends near 100 km; a satellite is hundreds of km away, which is one reason NTN broadcasts a common TA.
