title: 'Measurements: RSRP, RSRQ, SINR'
source: research
points:
- <b>RSRP</b> is the average power of one reference-signal resource element. LTE measures it on CRS; NR <b>SS-RSRP</b> on the SSS resource elements (inside the SMTC window); <b>CSI-RSRP</b> on configured CSI-RS.
- <b>RSSI</b> is the total received power in the measurement bandwidth, including interference and noise.
- <b>RSRQ</b> = N × RSRP / RSSI, where N is the number of RBs in the RSSI bandwidth. It falls as load and interference rise.
- <b>SINR</b> (SS-SINR in NR) is signal power over noise plus interference, per resource element.
- <i>Rule of thumb, not a spec value:</i> RSRP about −80 dBm strong, −100 fair, −110 weak, around −120 cell edge; SINR above 20 dB excellent, 13–20 good, 0–13 usable, below 0 poor.
- Good RSRP with bad SINR means interference. Bad RSRP with good SINR means a clean but weak, noise-limited link.
say: RSRP is coverage, SINR is quality. I always read them together and say which reference signal they came from.
trap: 'RSRP is per resource element, so it is far below total channel power: on a 5 MHz carrier (300 subcarriers) the gap is about 25 dB.'
