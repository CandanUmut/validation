title: "Measurements: RSRP, RSRQ, SINR"
source: v1
points:
  - "<b>RSRP</b>: average power of one reference signal resource element, in dBm. It is signal strength."
  - "<b>RSSI</b>: total received power in the measurement bandwidth, including interference and noise."
  - <b>RSRQ</b> = N x RSRP / RSSI (N = number of RBs). It is a quality measure that drops as load and interference rise.
  - "<b>SINR</b>: signal over interference plus noise. It is the best predictor of throughput."
  - "Rule-of-thumb RSRP: about -80 dBm strong, -100 fair, -110 weak, around -120 cell edge."
  - "Rule-of-thumb SINR: above 20 dB excellent, 13 to 20 good, 0 to 13 usable, below 0 poor."
  - Good RSRP with bad SINR means interference. Bad RSRP with good SINR means a clean but weak link (noise-limited).
say: RSRP tells me coverage, SINR tells me quality. I always read them together.
trap: RSRP is per resource element, so it is much lower than total channel power. For a full 5 MHz carrier the gap is about 25 dB.
