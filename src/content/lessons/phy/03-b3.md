title: Cell search and synchronization
source: v1
points:
  - The phone first finds <b>PSS</b> and <b>SSS</b> (primary and secondary sync signals). Together they give timing, frequency offset and the PCI.
  - "LTE: PSS/SSS repeat every 5 ms. The <b>MIB</b> is on PBCH with a 40 ms period and gives bandwidth, PHICH config and frame number."
  - "NR: PSS, SSS and PBCH are packed in an <b>SSB</b> block (4 symbols, 20 RBs). SSBs are sent in a burst, one per beam. The phone assumes a 20 ms period for initial search."
  - The NR MIB points to <b>CORESET 0</b> and search space 0, where the phone finds the DCI for <b>SIB1</b>.
  - After MIB and SIB1 the phone knows PLMN, tracking area, whether the cell is barred, minimum signal level, and the random access configuration.
  - A phone can only lock if the frequency error is within what its search can handle. A large uncorrected Doppler shift can make the cell invisible.
say: PSS and SSS give timing and cell ID, the MIB tells the phone where to find SIB1, and SIB1 tells it how to access the cell.
trap: If a phone never camps, check sync and SIB1 decoding before you look at RACH. No SIB1 means no RACH attempt at all.
