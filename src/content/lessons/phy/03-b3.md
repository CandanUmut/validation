title: Cell search and synchronization
source: research
points:
- 'The UE first finds <b>PSS</b> and <b>SSS</b>. Together they give the physical cell ID: 504 in LTE, <b>1008</b> in NR.'
- <b>LTE:</b> PSS and SSS are sent in subframes 0 and 5 (every 5 ms, FDD). The MIB is on PBCH over a 40 ms period and carries the bandwidth, PHICH config and frame number.
- <b>NR:</b> PSS, SSS and PBCH form an <b>SS/PBCH block (SSB)</b> of 4 symbols × 240 subcarriers (20 RBs), sent in bursts, one per beam.
- For initial cell selection the UE may assume SSB half-frames repeat every <b>2 frames (20 ms)</b>.
- 'The NR MIB tells the UE whether a CORESET for the Type0-PDCCH common search space exists, and where (<code>pdcch-ConfigSIB1</code>: <code>controlResourceSetZero</code>, <code>searchSpaceZero</code>). There it finds the DCI that schedules SIB1.'
- <i>In practice:</i> a UE only locks if its frequency error is within what its search tolerates; a large uncorrected Doppler shift can make a cell invisible.
say: PSS and SSS give timing and cell ID; the MIB says where SIB1's control channel is; SIB1 says how to access.
trap: No SIB1 means no random access at all. If a UE never camps, check SSB detection and SIB1 decoding before you look at RACH.
