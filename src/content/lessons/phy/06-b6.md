title: 'Link adaptation: CQI, MCS, BLER'
source: research
points:
- The UE reports <b>CQI</b> (0 to 15), <b>RI</b> (number of layers) and <b>PMI</b> (precoder).
- <b>Where the 10% comes from:</b> CQI is defined as the highest value at which the UE could receive a transport block with error probability <b>not exceeding 0.1</b>. With the low-latency CQI table (<code>table3</code>) the target is 0.00001.
- '<i>In practice:</i> the scheduler picks the MCS from CQI and corrects it with <b>outer-loop link adaptation</b>: up after ACKs, down after NACKs, to hold a BLER target (often about 10%). This loop is vendor implementation, not specified.'
- <i>In practice:</i> in uplink the gNB measures the UE's signal (DMRS or SRS) and picks the MCS itself.
- '<i>In practice:</i> good SINR with low MCS or high BLER: suspect wrong CQI reports, interference bursts, frequency or timing offset, RF path problems, or PA compression.'
- <i>In practice:</i> first-transmission BLER near 0% usually means the MCS is too cautious; residual BLER after all HARQ retransmissions should be near zero.
say: CQI is the UE's 10%-error promise; the scheduler turns it into an MCS and an outer loop holds the BLER target.
trap: Initial BLER and residual BLER are different numbers. Quote which one, and from which counter.
