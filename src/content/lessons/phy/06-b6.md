title: "Link adaptation: CQI, MCS, BLER"
source: v1
points:
  - The phone measures the downlink and reports <b>CQI</b> (0 to 15), <b>RI</b> (how many layers) and <b>PMI</b> (which precoder).
  - The scheduler picks an <b>MCS</b> so that the first transmission fails about <b>10%</b> of the time. That is the usual BLER target. HARQ recovers the rest.
  - <b>Outer-loop link adaptation (OLLA)</b> nudges the MCS up after ACKs and down after NACKs to hold the target.
  - "Uplink: the base station measures SINR on DMRS or SRS and picks the uplink MCS itself."
  - Good SINR but low MCS or high BLER? Suspect wrong CQI reports, interference bursts, frequency or timing offset, RF path problems, or PA compression.
  - BLER near 0% is not a win. It usually means the MCS is too conservative and throughput is left on the table.
say: CQI goes up, MCS comes down as a decision, and the outer loop holds BLER near ten percent.
trap: Initial BLER and residual BLER are different. Residual BLER (after all HARQ retransmissions) should be close to zero.
