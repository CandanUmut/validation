title: OFDM grid and numerology
source: v1
points:
  - "LTE: subcarrier spacing (SCS) is fixed at <b>15 kHz</b>. One subframe is 1 ms. One slot has 7 symbols, so 14 symbols per subframe (normal CP)."
  - "One <b>resource block (RB)</b> is 12 subcarriers. In LTE that is 180 kHz. LTE bandwidths: 1.4, 3, 5, 10, 15, 20 MHz give 6, 15, 25, 50, 75, 100 RBs."
  - "NR: SCS = 15 x 2^&mu; kHz. &mu;=0..3 gives 15, 30, 60, 120 kHz. A slot is always 14 symbols, so a higher SCS means a shorter slot (1 ms, 0.5 ms, 0.25 ms, 0.125 ms)."
  - <b>FR1</b> is 410 MHz to 7.125 GHz and uses 15, 30 or 60 kHz, up to 100 MHz bandwidth. <b>FR2</b> is mmWave (24.25 GHz and up) and uses 60 or 120 kHz, up to 400 MHz.
  - The <b>cyclic prefix (CP)</b> is a guard at the start of each symbol. It absorbs delay spread and small timing errors. LTE normal CP is about 4.7 &micro;s.
  - A <b>resource element (RE)</b> is one subcarrier in one symbol. It is the smallest unit and carries one modulation symbol.
say: LTE is locked to 15 kHz. NR scales the subcarrier spacing, which trades symbol length against tolerance to Doppler and phase noise.
trap: Wider SCS tolerates more Doppler but has a shorter CP, so it tolerates less timing error. Both matter on a satellite link.
