title: OFDM grid and numerology
source: research
points:
- <b>LTE:</b> subcarrier spacing 15 kHz, 10 ms frames of ten 1 ms subframes, 7 symbols per slot with normal CP (14 per subframe).
- A <b>resource block</b> is 12 subcarriers (180 kHz at 15 kHz). A <b>resource element</b> is one subcarrier in one symbol.
- LTE carriers have 6, 15, 25, 50, 75 or 100 RBs (the MIB's <code>dl-Bandwidth</code>), usually quoted as 1.4, 3, 5, 10, 15 and 20 MHz.
- '<b>NR:</b> subcarrier spacing = 15 × 2<sup>μ</sup> kHz. Release 17 defines μ = 0 to 6: 15, 30, 60, 120, 240, 480 and 960 kHz. Extended CP exists only at 60 kHz.'
- 'A slot is always <b>14 symbols</b> with normal CP (12 with extended CP), so slot length shrinks as spacing grows: 1, 0.5, 0.25, 0.125 ms …'
- <b>FR1</b> is 410 MHz to 7.125 GHz with 15, 30 or 60 kHz. Maximum channel 100 MHz, but only up to 50 MHz (270 RBs) at 15 kHz; 100 MHz at 30 kHz is 273 RBs.
- LTE normal CP is 144 Ts ≈ 4.7 µs (160 Ts ≈ 5.2 µs on the first symbol of a slot), with Ts = 1/(15000 × 2048) s.
say: LTE is fixed at 15 kHz. NR scales the spacing by powers of two; the slot stays 14 symbols, so it gets shorter.
trap: 'At 15 kHz NR cannot do 100 MHz: the maximum is 50 MHz. A 100 MHz FR1 test needs 30 or 60 kHz.'
