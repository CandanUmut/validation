title: 'The big picture: UE, RAN, core'
source: research
points:
- 'A mobile network has three parts: the <b>UE</b>, the <b>RAN</b> (base stations) and the <b>core</b> (subscriber, mobility, session and routing functions).'
- <b>LTE:</b> the base station is the <b>eNB</b>; the core is the <b>EPC</b> with the MME (signalling, mobility), the Serving Gateway (S-GW) and the PDN Gateway (P-GW) for user data.
- <b>5G:</b> the base station is the <b>gNB</b>; the core (5GC) has the AMF (access and mobility), SMF (sessions), UPF (user plane), UDM and AUSF (subscriber data, authentication).
- <b>LTE interfaces:</b> Uu (air), S1-MME (eNB–MME, S1AP), S1-U (eNB–S-GW, GTP-U), X2 (eNB–eNB).
- <b>5G reference points:</b> N2 between the RAN and the AMF (NGAP), N3 between the RAN and the UPF (GTP-U), Xn between gNBs. On the RAN side these are the NG-C and NG-U interfaces.
- 'RAN validation tests the eNB/gNB: PHY up to RRC towards the UE, and S1AP or NGAP towards the core.'
say: 'The eNB or gNB is the radio side: PHY up to RRC towards the UE, S1 or NG towards the core.'
trap: 'Keep the generations apart: MME, S-GW and P-GW are EPC; AMF, SMF and UPF are 5GC.'
