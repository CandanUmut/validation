title: "The big picture: UE, RAN, core"
source: v1
points:
  - "A mobile network has three parts: the <b>UE</b> (phone), the <b>RAN</b> (base stations), and the <b>core</b> (subscriber, session and routing functions)."
  - "<b>LTE:</b> the base station is the <b>eNB</b>. The core is the <b>EPC</b>: MME (signaling, mobility), SGW and PGW (user data), HSS (subscriber database)."
  - "<b>5G NR:</b> the base station is the <b>gNB</b>. The core is the <b>5GC</b>: AMF (access and mobility), SMF (sessions), UPF (user data), UDM and AUSF (subscriber data, authentication)."
  - "LTE interfaces: <code>Uu</code> (UE to eNB, the air interface), <code>S1-MME</code> (eNB to MME, protocol S1AP), <code>S1-U</code> (eNB to SGW, GTP-U), <code>X2</code> (eNB to eNB)."
  - "NR interfaces: <code>Uu</code>, <code>N2</code> (gNB to AMF, protocol NGAP), <code>N3</code> (gNB to UPF, GTP-U), <code>Xn</code> (gNB to gNB). N2 and N3 together are called NG."
  - "This role tests the RAN: the eNB/gNB software stack that speaks PHY, MAC, RLC, PDCP and RRC to the phone, and S1AP/NGAP to the core."
say: The eNB or gNB is the radio side of the network. It runs PHY up to RRC towards the phone and S1 or NG towards the core.
trap: Do not mix up the names. MME, SGW, PGW belong to LTE. AMF, SMF, UPF belong to 5G.
