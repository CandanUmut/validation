title: Inside a gNB, and where it sits on a satellite
source: research
points:
- <b>3GPP split:</b> the <b>gNB-CU</b> hosts RRC, SDAP and PDCP; the <b>gNB-DU</b> hosts RLC, MAC and PHY. They are joined by <code>F1</code>. The CU can be split again into CU-CP and CU-UP, joined by <code>E1</code>.
- One DU serves one or more cells; one cell belongs to only one DU.
- <b>O-RAN</b> (not 3GPP) splits the PHY further between an O-DU (high PHY) and an O-RU (low PHY, RF) over its 7.2x fronthaul.
- '<b>Satellites, Release 17:</b> the NTN payload is <b>transparent</b>: it forwards the radio signal between the UE (service link) and an NTN gateway on the ground (feeder link); the gNB is on the ground.'
- <i>In practice:</i> if base-station functions were on board (regenerative), the UE-to-gNB delay would be the service link only, not service plus feeder link.
say: '3GPP: CU has RRC, SDAP, PDCP; DU has RLC, MAC, PHY. Release 17 satellites are transparent repeaters with the gNB on the ground.'
