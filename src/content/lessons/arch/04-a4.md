title: Inside a gNB, and where it sits on a satellite
source: v1
points:
  - A gNB can be split into <b>CU</b> (RRC, PDCP), <b>DU</b> (RLC, MAC, upper PHY) and <b>RU</b> (lower PHY, RF). CU to DU is the <code>F1</code> interface.
  - "For satellites there are two designs. <b>Transparent</b> (bent pipe): the satellite only repeats the signal and the base station is on the ground. <b>Regenerative</b>: the base station, or part of it, flies on the satellite."
  - 3GPP Release 17 NTN covers the transparent design. Regenerative payloads are being added in later releases.
  - SpaceX has said publicly that each direct-to-cell satellite carries an LTE modem that works like a cell tower in space, with laser links back to the Starlink network. That means base station functions are on the satellite.
  - "Why it matters for testing: with the base station on the satellite, the delay between phone and base station is only the service link (a few milliseconds), not service link plus feeder link."
say: Transparent means the satellite is a repeater. Regenerative means the base station is on board. Starlink direct-to-cell describes itself as a cell tower in space.
trap: Do not claim to know SpaceX internal design. Say what is public, then reason from physics.
