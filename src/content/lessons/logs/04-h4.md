title: Network-side view
source: research
points:
- '<i>In practice:</i> when the base station is the product, read its own logs too: scheduler traces, PHY measurements (uplink SINR, timing and frequency offset), RRC and NGAP/S1AP traces, counters.'
- '<i>In practice:</i> the base station sees what the UE cannot: uplink quality, why the scheduler did not grant, whether a preamble was detected, what the core answered.'
- '<i>In practice:</i> the UE sees what the base station cannot: downlink quality, whether it decoded the DCI, its own power and timers.'
- <b>NGAP messages to know:</b> INITIAL UE MESSAGE (first NAS message to the AMF), INITIAL CONTEXT SETUP REQUEST, UE CONTEXT RELEASE COMMAND (with a cause), HANDOVER REQUIRED, PATH SWITCH REQUEST.
- <b>XnAP handover:</b> HANDOVER REQUEST, HANDOVER REQUEST ACKNOWLEDGE, SN STATUS TRANSFER, UE CONTEXT RELEASE.
- '<i>In practice:</i> root cause often needs both views: ''sent'' on one side and ''never received'' on the other localises the fault to the air link or the layers below.'
say: The two logs are two halves of one story. Where they disagree is usually where the bug is.
trap: 'Read the release cause in UE CONTEXT RELEASE COMMAND before anything else: it often names the reason directly.'
