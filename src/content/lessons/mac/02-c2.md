title: Scheduling and grants
source: research
points:
- The gNB scheduler decides, slot by slot, who gets which resource blocks and which MCS.
- 'Downlink: DCI on PDCCH, then PDSCH. Uplink: DCI grant, then PUSCH.'
- <b>LTE FDD timing is fixed:</b> PUSCH 4 subframes after the grant (k = 4), HARQ-ACK 4 subframes after the PDSCH.
- <b>NR timing is configured:</b> <code>K0</code> (DCI to PDSCH, 0–32 slots), <code>K1</code> (PDSCH to HARQ-ACK, from <code>dl-DataToUL-ACK</code>, 0–15), <code>K2</code> (DCI to PUSCH).
- <b>Uplink start:</b> with data but no grant, the UE sends a <b>scheduling request</b> on PUCCH; with a grant it reports its <b>buffer status (BSR)</b> and gets more.
- <b>Configured grants</b> (uplink) and semi-persistent scheduling (downlink) avoid a DCI per packet, as for voice.
- <i>In practice:</i> scheduler policies (round robin, max C/I, proportional fair) are vendor choices, not specified.
say: Downlink is assign then send. Uplink is request, grant, report the buffer, bigger grant.
trap: 'Slow uplink start is often the SR path: a long SR period, an undetected SR, or <code>sr-ProhibitTimer</code>.'
