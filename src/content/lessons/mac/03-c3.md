title: HARQ
source: research
points:
- '<b>HARQ</b> is fast retransmission in MAC with soft combining: the receiver keeps the failed attempt and combines it with the retransmission.'
- 'It runs as parallel stop-and-wait <b>processes</b>. LTE FDD: up to 8 downlink processes. NR: 8 by default, configurable from 2 to 16, and 32 from Release 17 (needed on long-delay links).'
- <b>NDI</b> (new data indicator) toggles for new data and stays the same for a retransmission.
- <b>Redundancy versions</b> send different parity bits each time; for PUSCH repetitions starting at RV 0 the order is 0, 2, 3, 1.
- 'LTE uplink HARQ is synchronous. LTE downlink and NR HARQ are asynchronous: the DCI names the process.'
- If HARQ gives up, RLC AM recovers the data with ARQ, more slowly.
- <i>In practice:</i> HARQ assumes a short round trip; on long-delay links the processes run out (HARQ stalling) unless you add processes or turn feedback off.
say: HARQ is the fast repair loop in MAC with soft combining; RLC ARQ is the slower safety net above it.
trap: BLER is counted on first transmissions. A link at 10% BLER with working HARQ loses almost nothing.
