title: Random access (RACH)
source: research
points:
- '<b>Triggers</b> (TS 38.300): initial access from idle, re-establishment, data arrival when uplink is not synchronised, uplink data with no PUCCH for SR, handover, SR failure, resume from inactive, other-SI request, beam failure recovery, consistent uplink LBT failure, small data transmission, positioning.'
- <b>Msg1:</b> a preamble on PRACH. NR defines <b>64 preambles in each PRACH occasion</b>, built from Zadoff-Chu sequences.
- <b>Msg2:</b> the random access response, addressed to RA-RNTI, inside <code>ra-ResponseWindow</code>. It carries a timing advance command, an uplink grant and a temporary C-RNTI.
- <b>Msg3:</b> the UE's identity on PUSCH (for example <code>RRCSetupRequest</code>).
- '<b>Msg4:</b> contention resolution: the UE must see its identity echoed before <code>ra-ContentionResolutionTimer</code> expires (LTE calls it <code>mac-ContentionResolutionTimer</code>).'
- <b>Contention-based</b> (UE picks a random preamble) or <b>contention-free</b> (network assigns one, used in handover and beam failure recovery).
- 'With contention-free access the procedure <b>ends at the RAR</b>: no Msg3 or Msg4. Without contention-free resources, an RSRP threshold makes the UE choose between 2-step and 4-step RACH.'
- <b>Power ramping:</b> with no RAR, the UE retries at <code>powerRampingStep</code> higher (0, 2, 4 or 6 dB), up to <code>preambleTransMax</code> (3 to 200 attempts). A step of 0 dB is a legal configuration.
- '<b>2-step RACH</b>: MSGA (preamble plus payload) and MSGB.'
say: Preamble, response with timing advance and grant, identity on Msg3, contention resolution on Msg4.
trap: <i>In practice:</i> the preamble format and <code>zeroCorrelationZoneConfig</code> limit how far away a preamble can be detected. Too small for the cell and distant UEs are never heard.
