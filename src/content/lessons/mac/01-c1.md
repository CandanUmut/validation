---
id: c1
module: mac
order: 1
title: Random access (RACH)
source: v1
points:
  - "Used for: first access, re-establishment, handover, uplink data when out of sync, scheduling request failure, beam failure recovery."
  - <b>Msg1:</b> the phone sends a <b>preamble</b> on PRACH. There are 64 preambles per cell, built from Zadoff-Chu sequences.
  - <b>Msg2:</b> the base station answers with a <b>random access response (RAR)</b>, addressed to RA-RNTI, inside <code>ra-ResponseWindow</code>. It carries the timing advance, an uplink grant and a temporary C-RNTI.
  - <b>Msg3:</b> the phone sends its identity on PUSCH (for example RRC Connection Request or RRCSetupRequest).
  - <b>Msg4:</b> contention resolution. The phone must see its own identity echoed before <code>mac-ContentionResolutionTimer</code> expires.
  - "<b>Contention-based</b> (CBRA): phone picks a random preamble. <b>Contention-free</b> (CFRA): network assigns one, used in handover."
  - <b>Power ramping:</b> if no RAR arrives, the phone raises power by <code>powerRampingStep</code> and tries again, up to <code>preambleTransMax</code>.
  - "NR Release 16 adds <b>2-step RACH</b>: MsgA (preamble + payload) and MsgB."
say: Preamble, response with timing advance and grant, identity on Msg3, contention resolution on Msg4.
trap: The preamble format and <code>zeroCorrelationZoneConfig</code> set the largest cell radius the base station can detect. Too small and far phones are never heard.
---
