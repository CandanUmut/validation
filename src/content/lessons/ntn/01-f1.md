title: "The physics: distance, speed, delay, Doppler"
source: v1
points:
  - A low Earth orbit satellite flies at about <b>7.7 km/s</b>, a few hundred kilometers up. SpaceX gives this speed in its own direct-to-cell update.
  - <b>Delay:</b> light covers 300 km per millisecond. At 550 km straight overhead the one-way delay is about 1.8 ms. At a low elevation the slant range can pass 1,500 km, about 5 ms.
  - <b>Delay changes fast:</b> as the satellite passes, the path length changes by kilometers per second. The timing advance must follow.
  - <b>Doppler:</b> shift = (v / c) x f x cos(angle). At 1.9 GHz and 7.7 km/s the worst case is about <b>48 kHz</b>. LTE subcarriers are only 15 kHz apart.
  - <b>Doppler rate:</b> the shift sweeps from positive to negative during a pass, by hundreds of Hz per second.
  - <b>Path loss:</b> FSPL(dB) = 32.45 + 20 log10(km) + 20 log10(MHz). At 550 km and 1900 MHz that is about 153 dB. A 5 km terrestrial link at the same frequency is about 112 dB.
  - <b>Phone limits:</b> 0.2 W transmit power and a low-gain antenna. The satellite needs a very large, sensitive antenna array to close the uplink.
say: Compared with a tower, a satellite link has about 40 dB more path loss, milliseconds of extra and changing delay, and Doppler several times the subcarrier spacing.
trap: Do not say 'latency is the main problem'. For LEO the delay is small. Changing delay, Doppler and link budget are the hard parts.
