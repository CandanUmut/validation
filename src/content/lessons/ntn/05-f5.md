title: What a validation engineer would test
source: research
points:
- <i>In practice:</i> <b>Access:</b> time from no service to registered; RACH success versus elevation angle; registration success across phone models.
- <i>In practice:</i> <b>Link quality:</b> residual frequency offset, timing advance distribution, BLER and MCS versus elevation, uplink power headroom.
- <i>In practice:</i> <b>Mobility:</b> handover success across beam and satellite changes; drop rate over a full pass; recovery time after a gap; conditional handover with time and location triggers.
- <i>In practice:</i> <b>Services:</b> SMS delivery time and success, data session setup, throughput per beam, voice where offered.
- <i>In practice:</i> <b>Interoperability:</b> many phone models and chipsets, because each tolerates Doppler and timing differently; for NR-NTN, GNSS fix time and ephemeris validity handling.
- <i>In practice:</i> <b>Coexistence:</b> the UE should prefer the terrestrial network and return to it quickly. Test the coverage edges.
- <i>In practice:</i> <b>Lab first:</b> a channel emulator plays satellite delay and Doppler profiles so a pass can be replayed on the bench; then confirm under real satellites.
say: I test access, link quality, mobility and services across many phone models, first with emulated passes in the lab and then under real satellites.
trap: One pass is not a sample. Results depend on elevation, beam position and phone model, so plan enough passes to see the spread.
