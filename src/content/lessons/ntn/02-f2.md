title: "Two ways to do it: NTN-aware phones or unmodified phones"
source: v1
points:
  - <b>3GPP NTN (Release 17 and later):</b> the phone knows it is talking to a satellite. It has GNSS, reads the satellite orbit from system information, and corrects its own timing and frequency before it transmits.
  - <b>Direct-to-cell with unmodified LTE phones:</b> the phone thinks it sees a normal tower. All the correction must happen on the network side.
  - SpaceX states that its service works with unmodified LTE phones, using partner operator LTE spectrum in the 1.6 to 2.7 GHz range, and that the satellite must handle Doppler and timing so that LTE still works.
  - "Network-side tricks, from first principles: pre-shift the downlink frequency and timing so they are correct at the beam center. Then every phone in the beam only sees the small leftover (residual) error."
  - Smaller beams mean smaller residual Doppler and delay spread across the beam, but more beams and more handovers.
  - SpaceX has also said that altitude, beam size, elevation angle and satellite count were chosen to sit just at the edge of where LTE works. So margins are thin, and validation has to find the edges.
  - Industry reports say Starlink plans to add NR-NTN as phone chipsets with Release 17 support become common.
say: With NTN-aware phones the phone compensates. With unmodified phones the satellite must make itself look like a normal cell, and only the residual error is left for the phone.
trap: Details of SpaceX's implementation are not public. Label your reasoning as reasoning. Interviewers respect that more than a confident guess.
