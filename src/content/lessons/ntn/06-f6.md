points:
  - <b>Received power (dBm)</b> = transmit power + transmit antenna gain - path loss - other losses + receive antenna gain.
  - <b>Noise floor (dBm)</b> = -174 + 10 log10(bandwidth in Hz) + noise figure.
  - <b>SNR</b> = received power - noise floor. Compare it with the SNR needed for the lowest MCS.
  - "Uplink example for one RB (180 kHz): phone 23 dBm, phone antenna about 0 dBi, path loss about 160 dB at low elevation. That leaves -137 dBm before satellite antenna gain."
  - Noise in 180 kHz is -174 + 52.6 = -121.4 dBm, plus the receiver noise figure. So without antenna gain the signal is far below noise.
  - The satellite antenna must supply the missing tens of dB. SpaceX describes phased arrays of about 2.7 m by 2.3 m for this.
  - "Ways to help the uplink: fewer RBs (same power in less bandwidth), low MCS, repetitions, HARQ combining."
say: I add up powers and gains, subtract path loss, compare with the noise floor, and see how many dB of margin are left.
trap: Giving the phone fewer RBs raises SNR per RB but lowers the data rate. That is the uplink trade-off in one sentence.
