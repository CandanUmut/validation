points:
- <b>Received power (dBm)</b> = transmit power + transmit antenna gain − path loss − other losses + receive antenna gain.
- <b>Noise floor (dBm)</b> = −174 + 10·log10(bandwidth in Hz) + noise figure.
- <b>SNR</b> = received power − noise floor. Compare it with the SNR needed for the lowest usable MCS.
- '<i>Calculation:</i> uplink, one RB (180 kHz): 23 dBm from the phone, about 0 dBi antenna, 163 dB path loss at 10° elevation → −140 dBm before the satellite''s antenna gain.'
- '<i>Calculation:</i> noise in 180 kHz is −174 + 52.6 = −121.4 dBm plus the receiver noise figure. Without antenna gain the signal is about 20 dB below noise: the satellite antenna must supply that and more.'
- 'Ways to help the uplink: fewer RBs (same power in less bandwidth), low MCS, repetitions, HARQ combining.'
say: I add powers and gains, subtract path loss, compare with the noise floor, and see how many dB of margin are left.
trap: Fewer RBs raise SNR per RB but lower the data rate. That is the uplink trade-off in one sentence.
