title: What breaks, layer by layer
source: research
points:
- <i>Reasoning, not a spec:</i> <b>Sync:</b> uncorrected Doppler can put PSS/SSS outside the UE's search range, so it never sees the cell.
- <i>Reasoning, not a spec:</i> <b>PHY:</b> residual frequency offset causes inter-carrier interference; low SINR forces low MCS; the Doppler rate stresses the UE's frequency tracking.
- <i>Reasoning, not a spec:</i> <b>PRACH:</b> timing uncertainty across a wide beam can exceed what the preamble format tolerates; the gNB must search a wider window.
- <i>Reasoning, not a spec:</i> <b>Timing advance:</b> the absolute delay is beyond the terrestrial range and drifts quickly; Release 17 handles it with UE pre-compensation plus Common TA.
- <b>MAC:</b> LTE FDD's fixed n+4 HARQ timing and the terrestrial RAR window and contention timer all assume a short round trip; NR-NTN extends them explicitly.
- <i>Reasoning, not a spec:</i> <b>RLC/PDCP:</b> timers tuned for terrestrial HARQ delay fire too early; NR-NTN adds longer values.
- <i>Reasoning, not a spec:</i> <b>RRC:</b> all UEs in a beam need to move at about the same time as the satellite passes; failure timers must tolerate short gaps.
- <i>Reasoning, not a spec:</i> <b>NAS/core:</b> PLMN selection decides when a UE uses the satellite at all; tracking areas must stay meaningful while beams sweep the ground (in NTN, a tracking area is a fixed geographical area).
say: 'I go up the stack: can it sync, can it RACH, does timing hold, does HARQ timing fit, do layer 2 timers fit, does mobility survive a pass.'
trap: Each layer's default timer was chosen for a few-millisecond round trip. When something times out on a satellite link, first check whether the timer allows for the RTT.
