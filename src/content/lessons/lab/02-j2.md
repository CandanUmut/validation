points:
- <i>In practice:</i> <b>attenuators</b> (fixed and programmable) set path loss and simulate distance or fading.
- <i>In practice:</i> <b>splitters and combiners:</b> a 2-way splitter loses a bit over 3 dB per branch (3 dB from the split, plus insertion loss).
- <i>In practice:</i> <b>shield boxes</b> isolate a phone from live networks and from other benches.
- <i>In practice:</i> <b>cables and connectors</b> (SMA, N) all add loss and can fail; use the right torque.
- <i>In practice:</i> <b>circulators, duplexers, filters</b> separate transmit and receive paths and bands.
- <i>In practice:</i> <b>channel emulators</b> add fading, delay and Doppler repeatably.
- <i>In practice:</i> <b>instruments:</b> spectrum analyser (what is on the air), signal generator, power meter, vector network analyser (cable and component loss).
- <i>In practice:</i> <b>reference clock:</b> instruments share a 10 MHz reference so their frequencies agree.
say: Attenuators set the link, shield boxes keep it clean, and a spectrum analyser tells me what is really on the cable.
trap: An unshared reference clock shows up as a frequency offset that looks like a UE or gNB bug.
