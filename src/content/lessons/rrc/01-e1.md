title: RRC states
source: research
points:
- '<b>NR</b> has three states: <code>RRC_IDLE</code> (no RRC connection), <code>RRC_CONNECTED</code>, and <code>RRC_INACTIVE</code>, where the connection is suspended and the UE keeps its <b>UE Inactive AS context</b>.'
- <b>LTE</b> has <code>RRC_IDLE</code> and <code>RRC_CONNECTED</code>. An LTE UE connected to the <b>5G core</b> can also be in <code>RRC_INACTIVE</code>.
- <b>Idle:</b> the UE moves by itself (cell reselection, under network rules), monitors paging for core-network paging with its 5G-S-TMSI, measures neighbours and reads system information.
- <b>Inactive:</b> the same UE-controlled mobility, plus the network configures a <b>RAN notification area</b>. The UE is also paged by the RAN using its <code>fullI-RNTI</code>, and sends an area update when it leaves that area or periodically.
- <b>Connected:</b> the network controls mobility (handover), data flows, and carrier aggregation or dual connectivity can be used.
- 'How long the gNB keeps an idle-looking connection is its own policy. NR can also give the UE a <code>dataInactivityTimer</code> (1 to 180 s): when it expires, the UE itself goes to <code>RRC_IDLE</code> with release cause ''RRC connection failure''.'
say: 'Idle: the UE manages itself and has no RRC connection. Connected: the network manages it. Inactive: suspended, context kept, paged by the RAN and resumed quickly.'
trap: A UE that drops to idle after a quiet period is not always the gNB releasing it. Check whether <code>dataInactivityTimer</code> was configured before you file a bug.
