title: Radio link failure and re-establishment
source: v1
points:
  - "RLF triggers: <b>T310 expiry</b>, <b>random access failure</b> (max preambles), <b>RLC maximum retransmissions</b>, handover failure (T304)."
  - After RLF the phone starts <b>T311</b>, finds a cell, and sends an <b>RRC re-establishment request</b> with a cause (otherFailure, handoverFailure, reconfigurationFailure).
  - Re-establishment works only if security was active and the target cell has (or can fetch) the phone's context.
  - If it fails, the phone goes to idle and NAS recovers with a service request or registration. The user sees a longer gap.
  - RLF reports and re-establishment counters are the raw data behind the 'drop rate' KPI.
say: A failure is declared by T310, RACH, or RLC. Then the phone tries to re-establish, and if that fails it starts over from idle.
trap: A drop is not always a radio problem. An unexpected RRC release from the network also ends the call. Check who ended it.
