title: Radio link failure and re-establishment
source: research
points:
- <b>RLF is detected on:</b> T310 expiry, T312 expiry, a random access problem (only when T300, T301, T304, T311 and T319 are not running), RLC reaching maximum retransmissions, or consistent uplink LBT failure.
- Handover failure (T304 expiry) is a separate trigger, 'reconfiguration with sync failure', but it also leads to re-establishment.
- 'Other re-establishment triggers: integrity check failure on SRB1 or SRB2, RRC reconfiguration failure, mobility-from-NR failure.'
- The UE starts <b>T311</b>, selects a cell and sends <code>RRCReestablishmentRequest</code> with cause <code>reconfigurationFailure</code>, <code>handoverFailure</code> or <code>otherFailure</code>.
- 'Without active AS security there is no re-establishment: the UE goes straight to <code>RRC_IDLE</code>.'
- With <code>t316</code> configured and dual connectivity, an MCG failure can first be reported through the SCG instead (fast MCG recovery).
- If re-establishment fails, the UE ends up in idle and NAS recovers. RLF reports are the raw data behind drop-rate KPIs.
say: Failure comes from T310, T312, RACH, RLC or LBT; handover failure has its own path. Then re-establish within T311, and only if security was active.
trap: 'A random access failure during handover is not an RLF: T304 is running. Read which timer was active before you label the failure.'
